# AI Enablement, Adoption, and Operating Design

## Working target

Pursue Director and Senior Director roles that own some combination of AI enablement, adoption, use-case discovery, workflow and product design, pilot portfolios, governance, and decision support in complex organizations.

Relevant role labels include AI Enablement, AI Adoption, AI Transformation, Applied AI, AI Strategy and Operations, AI Portfolio Leadership, and Digital Transformation with a defined AI mandate. Titles remain inconsistent across the market, so role scope matters more than title alone.

## Resume position

**Senior Director of AI Strategy** with a business-side focus on AI enablement, practical workflow design, and responsible adoption. Doug translates emerging AI capability into decision-ready strategy, staff-facing guidance, pilots, and reusable methods.

This position draws on a longer operating pattern from product implementation: diagnose unclear or failing work systems, make the problem visible, translate user and business needs into a workable operating model, and build the conditions for people to use it.

## Strong evidence themes

- AI discovery and strategy materials that framed priorities, pilots, governance, and operating conditions.
- A structured ChatGPT pilot that produced documented adoption findings and executive-facing summaries.
- Staff enablement through practical workshops, all-staff sessions, an AI Hub, office hours, and guidance.
- Governance, responsible-use, and workflow-review materials that distinguish individual experimentation from shared or higher-consequence work.
- Earlier product-implementation leadership in service operations, business-side product work, capacity and role design, remediation, and people development.

## Target-role boundaries

The target excludes roles whose core accountability is ML engineering, production model operations, cloud or AI-platform architecture, or software delivery leadership. Doug is not positioning himself as an engineer.

The target also avoids roles limited to training delivery without authority over use-case selection, enablement priorities, governance, or the path from pilots to operating practice. A suitable role has a named executive sponsor, clear decision rights, and a way to measure behavior or workflow change.
