# Director of Product Implementation Chapter Stocktake

## Current read

The recovered record supports a coherent leadership story. Doug grew a small data-request and portal-support function into a broader implementation capability that managed demand, product feedback, platform adoption, specialized roles, and staff development. The work moved across three connected layers: an operating system for handling demand, a business-side product function for shaping portal delivery, and a team model that developed internal people as the scope increased.

This is the right frame for the Director of Product Implementation chapter. The evidence does not support a claim that every capability or role change produced a measured business result. It does support a more credible statement: Doug repeatedly recognized an operating constraint, designed a way to handle it, recruited or developed capacity, and made the function more structured as the organization’s demands grew.

## What the evidence supports

### 1. Built an operating system for distributed implementation work

- **Strong evidence:** The 2016 through 2024 queue archive establishes an enduring centralized reporting surface for case volume, request types, owners, monthly intake, and related work. The 2020 supervisor review and later operating drafts corroborate a maturing implementation function.
- **Detailed account:** Doug recognized that email-based request and approval handling could not provide the history, visibility, planning signal, or approval trail that the organization needed. He led creation of the queue and team model around it.
- **Career implication:** This is a credible operations-design story. The safe claim is about building a centralized intake and reporting system for a distributed service function. It is premature to claim a specific efficiency gain.

### 2. Created business-side product and delivery capacity

- **Strong evidence:** The 2021 V4 deck, later Jira records, and the scenarios-and-drivers planning record establish a product-operations model involving field feedback, early testing, defect classification, implementation, and continuing education.
- **Detailed account:** Doug moved bug and development work into his team, introduced Scrum practices, and made the case for Joe Carroll’s dedicated Product Owner role.
- **Career implication:** This is a transferable product-operations story. The safe claim is about building an internal function that translated user demand into prioritization, feedback, and enablement for a separately organized delivery partner.

### 3. Built roles and developed a leadership bench

- **Strong evidence:** Individual records support several later role paths: Ross’s documented RDA-to-Implementation Manager progression, Joe’s Product Owner scope and later supervision, Rebecca’s EPS Product Owner role, Emily’s technical-training role, and the 2025 succession work around Mitch’s data-platform role. A 2020 supervisor review corroborates Doug’s broader people-development work.
- **Detailed account:** Doug reports hiring and developing a core group through coordinator, RDA, manager, product-owner, and senior implementation roles. He reports managing Ross for about ten years and working to retain high-value staff as responsibilities expanded.
- **Career implication:** This is a people-leadership story, not merely a staffing history. The supportable claim is that Doug built and developed an implementation bench as the operating model became more specialized. Individual performance actions, compensation, departures, and PIPs stay private.

### 4. Managed continuity when concentrated knowledge left the team

- **Strong evidence:** Doug-authored April 2025 knowledge capture for Mitch establishes a structured review of data-platform workflow, constraints, and successor needs. The product-ownership record also shows a later business case and contractor bridge as direct recovery targets.
- **Detailed account:** Doug reports taking over Mitch after Joe left, helping build the Snowflake backfill case after Mitch’s departure, and using Joe temporarily as a contractor while Product Owner continuity was arranged.
- **Career implication:** This supports an operational-continuity and succession-planning theme. Do not claim final hiring outcomes, approval authority, or the causes of departures until primary records are reviewed.

### 5. Tried to turn one-time portal training into a continuing-adoption model

- **Strong evidence:** The 2025 Portal Knowledge working-group document and a live Planner board establish a cross-functional adoption effort. The detailed board has 39 tasks across onboarding, training, role expectations, adoption, and discovery. Nine tasks are marked complete and five in progress.
- **Detailed account:** Doug reports initiating the group and Planner project to address weak knowledge retention beyond the roughly four-hour onboarding experience. He reports that the initiative later lost focus as E&T reorganized and attention shifted toward a possible CRM replacement.
- **Career implication:** This is evidence of diagnosing an enablement problem and forming a structured intervention. It is not a sustained-adoption success story.

### 6. Addressed a material asset-control failure

- **Strong evidence:** A billing workbook records 28 monthly snapshots from September 2021 through December 2023, with pooled service charges totaling about $1.09 million and line count declining from 3,493 to 1,556. A reconciliation workbook records 2,269 billed pooled lines against 1,335 asset-tracked devices, a 934-line gap. It also records 712 cancellation requests and 222 remaining lines to locate or cancel. A project plan documents cleanup work, carrier-line and device-management actions, responsibility assignments, and a planned vendor-managed transition.
- **Detailed account:** Doug reports handling the remediation project end to end. The work addressed an inherited fleet-control failure, concurrent Verizon billing issues, lost inventory, and the absence of an accountability policy. He reports diagnosing the problem, locating devices, defining ownership and approval flow, selecting the vendor-managed model, and directing cleanup.
- **Career implication:** This is a governance-and-remediation story. It shows the same recurring judgment pattern as the rest of the chapter: make a hidden operating failure visible, create clear process and ownership, and build an operating model that can prevent recurrence. Do not claim realized savings or completed remediation until an accountability and transition record is recovered.
