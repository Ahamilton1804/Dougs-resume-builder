# DPI-001 Evidence Addendum

## Regional Data Analyst work and team formation

Doug reports that, as a Regional Data Analyst, he worked on a small central team serving the organization through the i360 portal. The team processed data requests for field walkbooks and phone work, trained staff on portal use, and advised requesters on the available data. He describes the underlying data as including political, voting-history, modeled, and survey-tag information. This operational description is a detailed account and includes sensitive data context that should remain private.

Doug reports that the team began with three people, then operated with two after one colleague left. After a new manager arrived and the team hired a third person, the manager asked Doug to lead the team. He agreed and moved into the Implementation Manager role. The exact personnel timeline, formal decision record, and title-change documentation remain to be recovered.

## Queue origin and operating rationale

Doug reports that incoming requests and required approvals initially arrived and were managed through individual email inboxes. He identified the need for a shared queue so the team could centralize requests, manage approvals, retain a usable history, understand demand patterns, and support capacity planning. He reports that the queue also gave reviewers, including Legal, a single record for request decisions rather than requiring thread reconstruction from email.

This is a detailed account of the operating problem, Doug's role, and the intended value of the queue. It does not by itself establish who approved the system, its implementation date, which approval groups used it, or a measured improvement in turnaround time, planning accuracy, or headcount.

The dashboard is a case-management reporting surface: it displays closed cases by owner, cases opened by month, breakdowns by request type, and a total case card. It also displays a separate card for i360 universes assembled. The screenshots establish that the queue produced historical operational reporting by 2016 and continued through 2024.

The screenshots also show request types such as phone campaigns, mail lists, doors, vendor calls, imports or updates, bug reports, texting, counts, data visualizations, and development requests. Category labels and dashboard totals can change over time, so do not attribute each category, its introduction, or later volume increases to Doug without more direct evidence.

The screenshots are a 2025 archive of historical dashboard views, rather than contemporaneous 2016 to 2024 exports. They provide strong visual corroboration of the reporting model and recorded totals. A raw export, reporting definition, or data dictionary would be needed to audit the totals further.

## What this source supports

- Doug served in a central data-support role before becoming an Implementation Manager.
- A structured case-management queue existed by 2016 and supported organization-wide request tracking, reporting, and owner-level workload visibility.
- The system recorded annual case volume, request type, monthly intake, and i360 universe assembly volume over at least nine years.
- The queue's historical reporting created an operational basis for demand and capacity discussions.
