# DPI-001 Evidence Addendum

## Regional Data Analyst work and team formation

Doug reports that he was AFP's first Regional Data Analyst, reporting with the early RDA staff to John Pike before a formal data team existed. Around the queue's early formation, the RDA role moved the work beyond request execution toward consultation: helping teams consider how available data could advance their goals and projects. The team processed data requests for field walkbooks and phone work, trained staff on portal use, and advised requesters on the available data. This operational description is a detailed account and includes sensitive data context that should remain private.

Doug reports that the portal combined political and consumer information. He treated individual observations as context that required interpretation, since a signal could have several plausible underlying explanations. This was part of the RDA role's advisory premise, rather than a claim that any individual data point established a fact about a person. The examples and underlying data remain private.

Doug reports that the team began with three people, then operated with two after one colleague left. After a new manager arrived and the team hired a third person, the manager asked Doug to lead the team. He agreed and moved into the Implementation Manager role. The exact personnel timeline, formal decision record, and title-change documentation remain to be recovered.

## Queue origin and operating rationale

Doug reports that incoming requests and required approvals initially arrived and were managed through individual email inboxes. He identified the need for a shared queue so the team could centralize requests, manage approvals, retain a usable history, understand demand patterns, and support capacity planning. He reports that the queue also gave reviewers, including Legal, a single record for request decisions rather than requiring thread reconstruction from email.

This is a detailed account of the operating problem, Doug's role, and the intended value of the queue. It does not by itself establish who approved the system, its implementation date, which approval groups used it, or a measured improvement in turnaround time, planning accuracy, or headcount.

Doug reports that the first build was a basic Salesforce intake form created with the Salesforce team. Form fields fed the dashboard dimensions. He reports that later operations required clearer request definitions. For example, a request called a "new survey" could refer to a genuinely new survey requiring approval or an already approved survey reused in a different legislative area. Treating both as new work inflated new-survey volume and distorted later routing decisions. This is a detailed account of Doug's role in improving the operating taxonomy and routing model.

## Role specialization and team development

Doug reports that, during this early period, the organization separated the Regional Data Analyst role from data-coordination duties and hired staff focused on queue operations. In the early coordinator cohort, Ross Gardiner was the first hire, Andrew Gherardini was the second, and Joe Carroll was the third. Ross joined from a Test and Learn internship-like role, and Andrew had been an operations intern. All three monitored and completed requests from state teams. Doug reports that he later developed several long-tenured employees from intern-level roles into team-lead responsibilities. The personnel-development outcome and the exact timing of role separation remain detailed account pending role-change, hiring, or reporting-line records.

Doug reports that the team initially added another RDA while he continued to lead the team and support assigned states as an RDA himself. Rising demand made it increasingly difficult to meet both sets of responsibilities. He recalls an HR conversation that framed the choice plainly: he could continue as an RDA or manage the team, but could not sustain both. He then focused on managing the RDAs and coordinators. He describes the team's demand as closely tied to political cycles, requiring both administrative management and ongoing direction about what the team should do next. This is a detailed account pending staffing, role-description, or reporting-line records.

A user-supplied screenshot of former employee Joe Carroll's self-authored LinkedIn profile provides limited corroboration of the role model. It describes Data Coordinator work from June 2017 through June 2018 that included field-request management, survey and list production, data-quality work, user training, and support for Regional Data Analysts. His later profile entries describe Senior Data Coordinator, Regional Data Analyst, Product Owner, and Deputy Director roles. This is a useful lead on the function's scope and career progression. It does not establish that Doug created the roles, managed every listed responsibility, or caused Joe's advancement.

## Merger and consolidation account

Doug reports that, during a merger period he tentatively places around 2017, AFP, CVA, Libre, and Generation Opportunity each had separate data-support functions. He recalls that those other functions could not point to comparable activity, service-level, or workload measures. He reports that the queue's activity reporting allowed John Pike to explain the team's work during consolidation, after which the team retained its function and added one headcount while other data staff were released. He further recalls that CVA and Libre did much of their work in Salesforce, with inconsistent operating practices and limited usable reporting. This is a detailed account involving sensitive personnel and cross-entity consolidation information. The date, organizational decisions, relative staffing, system conditions, and causal connection to the team's retention require contemporaneous records before any use outside the private evidence record.

Doug reports that RDA field work expanded from portal navigation and technical training into consultative support: helping state teams identify how the available data and tools could support their objectives. This remains a detailed account pending training material, state feedback, or contemporaneous role documentation.

## Field-collection audit and data-integrity work

Doug tentatively places this work in 2018. He reports that the team gained visibility into collection points associated with door-to-door activity and used that visibility to audit field collection. He reports that the review surfaced suspected fraud and poor-quality data, requiring difficult conversations with state teams and responses to concerned field staff. In one reported case, the audit evidence indicated that a paid staff member had not committed the conduct attributed to them because another person had used that staff member's login. The account describes a data-integrity and investigative capability, as well as a corrective use that protected an individual from an incorrect accusation.

This is a detailed account. It does not establish the capability's date, technical source, review criteria, number of incidents, investigative finding in any particular case, or Doug's precise role in each review. The underlying activity, staff identities, credentials, locations, and incident records are sensitive. Recover a contemporaneous audit guide, capability announcement, incident summary with sensitive material removed, or decision record before treating this as an external claim.

A SharePoint help article published August 7, 2026, `Grassroots Performance Check for Doors`, documents a current dashboard designed to identify potential fraud in door activity. It describes organization, state, volunteer, and coordinator filters; distance, time, survey-distribution, and location indicators; a sorting grid for records requiring investigation; and map views that compare the household location with the volunteer's captured response location. Its route-playback feature states that it displays collection points as captured and shows the volunteer's walking path. This directly establishes the later existence and operating purpose of a collection-point and field-performance review capability. It does not establish when those functions first became available, whether the 2026 dashboard is technically the same system Doug remembers, his role in its creation or operation, or any historical investigation outcome.
