## Year One working narrative

Doug entered an underbuilt AI strategy role with broad expectations and unclear decision rights. He chose to create an initial operating foundation through discovery, a strategy point of view, pilot learning, staff enablement, guidance, and early governance work. The intended progression was:

1. Understand recurring work problems and adoption conditions.
2. Give staff a practical, responsible path for using available enterprise tools.
3. Capture experiments and recurring friction in a way that could surface stronger opportunities.
4. Move the most promising opportunities toward a named sponsor, owner, decision, test, and measure.

This is Doug's detailed account of the role's logic. It does not establish that AFP approved the model, adopted the recommendations, or achieved organization-wide AI maturity.

## Operating judgments to preserve

- Access to AI tools is a learning opportunity. It does not prove adoption, value, or staff capability.
- Problem clarity, relevant context, human judgment, and a way to assess the result matter more than generic tool fluency.
- Individual experimentation can remain lightweight when it stays within clear guardrails. Shared, persistent, higher-consequence, public-facing, or data-dependent work needs an owner, a defined scope, and appropriate review.
- Workshops and office hours should start with actual work problems. General demonstrations are weaker than a concrete workflow, desired outcome, relevant context, and a quality check.
- Discovery can identify patterns and possible opportunities. It cannot substitute for decision rights, sponsorship, resourcing, or an accountable operating owner.
- An AI strategy function should help create convergence across distributed experimentation. It should not become a bottleneck or claim delivery responsibility for systems owned elsewhere.
- A small set of sponsored opportunities with decisions and measures is more valuable than a large inventory of disconnected pilots or interesting concepts.

## Work themes that shaped the self-review

### Strategy and discovery

Doug developed strategy materials and a longer-horizon point of view after cross-functional discovery. He viewed the framework as an aspirational system and a way to test, revise, defer, or stop potential capabilities. The work was designed to create a shared frame for AI, not a fixed technical implementation plan.

### Practical enablement

Doug used workshops, an all-staff session, office hours, examples, and reusable Skills to help staff move from generic questions about AI toward defined work problems. His coaching pattern centered on role, objective, context, output, and human judgment. Early participant reactions and session records support direct engagement. Sustained behavior change remains unmeasured.

### Governance and responsible use

Doug created guidance and governance materials to connect practical use with accountability, data handling, decision rights, and escalation. The intended posture was enabling rather than restrictive: clear guardrails should let people act responsibly without waiting for a central approval process. The materials received substantive Legal review. Formal approval, publication, ownership, and staff uptake remain unresolved.

### Portfolio and operating-model thinking

Doug sees AI work as three connected lanes: staff literacy and use, experimentation and learning, and enterprise priorities. The associated working-group concept was intended to surface examples and patterns from across AFP, then route larger opportunities to the person who could decide and own them. The record does not establish that this became a durable program or that leadership accepted its enterprise-priority model.

### Strategic intelligence and research prototypes

Doug explored source-backed research, intelligence, and agentic workflow concepts because they could make complex work more tractable while retaining provenance and human review. These are proofs of concept and exploratory designs. Do not characterize them as adopted systems, delivered intelligence products, or completed political programs.

## Role constraints and development insight

Doug's core development insight is that the role needs a clearer convergence and handoff path after discovery. He can increase effectiveness by making three distinctions explicit:

1. Work that teams can conduct independently within established guidance.
2. Experiments or recurring friction that should enter a shared learning and triage view.
3. Opportunities that require a sponsor, a decision, an owner, or another function's delivery capacity.

This is a role-design and operating-model observation. It should not be used to attribute motive, indifference, or failure to specific leaders or departments.
