# DPI-004 Evidence Addendum

## 2017-2018 platform expansion

Doug tentatively places an organizational merger in 2017. He reports that the portal's parent-child architecture was created to operate several organizations within one shared system. AFP was the parent organization, with CVA, Libre, and Generation Opportunity configured as child organizations. The model was intended to segment volunteers for organization-specific work while allowing appropriate cross-survey participation. Doug reports that this created more complicated data and entity treatment because the organizations worked across different tax statuses and purposes.

This is a detailed account. It identifies a platform and operating-design problem, rather than a verified technical specification or a public description of entity practices. The merger date, architecture design, ownership, approval path, data rules, and exact legal treatment remain unrecovered. Keep all entity, tax, volunteer, and system details private.

## Tag governance and import operations

Doug reports that the portal accumulated a large number of survey-specific tags because each effort created its own tags and had little operating incentive to reuse or govern them after the immediate program ended. He recalls that the accumulated volume, roughly 20,000 tags, contributed to system performance pressure. The team's response included moving older tags into year-based structures and consolidating future tags into issue categories, so the system could retain usable historical context without continuing unchecked tag growth.

Doug also reports that the team developed an import process for vendor-provided records that needed to update portal data. This was part of the platform's growing implementation and data-management burden.

These are detailed accounts. The approximate tag count, performance effect, migration sequence, taxonomy, import controls, and Doug's precise decision rights need source records before they support external claims or quantitative statements.

## Volunteer and digital-engagement capabilities

Doug reports that Volunteer Management emerged around 2017 or 2018 to help teams manage volunteers and prospective volunteers. He recalls that iVolunteer followed in 2018 as a way to engage activists who had taken digital actions, such as online petitions or letters to the editor. Together, these capabilities expanded the portal beyond traditional door and phone programs into volunteer and digital-engagement workflows.

This is a detailed account. The product names, approximate sequence, intended purpose, and examples are useful recovery leads. Launch dates, user adoption, Doug's role, data flows, and results remain unverified.

## What this source supports

- Doug's account identifies 2017 and 2018 as a period when the implementation function expanded from request execution and portal training into multi-entity platform design, data governance, imports, volunteer management, and digital engagement.
