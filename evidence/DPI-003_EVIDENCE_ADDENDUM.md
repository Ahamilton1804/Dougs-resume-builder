# DPI-003 Evidence Addendum

## Portal product ownership and delivery operations

Doug reports that bug and development intake for the i360 portal moved to his team after the existing tracking process was not producing a usable operating system. Andrew Gherardini initially took on the work, then Joe Carroll assumed it. Doug reports that he began introducing Scrum practices and made the case to his supervisor and i360 for a dedicated Product Owner role for Joe. The intended role would convert user needs and defect reports into planned, prioritized work with a technical delivery partner, rather than leaving the work as informal ticket handling. Joe later held the Product Owner title full time.

This is a detailed account of the transition, Doug's advocacy, and the role-design decision. Doug further reports that Joe held the Product Owner role through a departure he tentatively places in 2025, then later returned under a different title. Joe's self-authored LinkedIn profile places the Product Owner role from May 2021 through February 2025 and contains later overlapping entries. It corroborates the role's later existence and stated scope. It does not independently establish who proposed the role, the departure and rehire dates, the title sequence, or Doug's role in the decision.

## Role expansion and organizational justification

Doug reports that the Regional Data Analyst role expanded and became more specialized as demand, platform scope, and the number of supported activities grew. He recalls that creating the Product Owner role required repeated explanation of why a grassroots organization needed a technical business role at all. The internal question was whether central IT could handle the work. Doug's account is that the Product Owner role held the business side of the interface: field and operational context, user demand, prioritization, requirements quality, and feedback on whether a delivery met the actual need. It was meant to give the delivery partner a clear, accountable business counterpart.

This is a detailed account of the operating rationale and organizational resistance. It should guide recovery, not become an external claim about any individual or organization. The key recovery task is a year-by-year account of role expansion, supported by dated artifacts, rather than one static description of RDA work.

## Documented delivery system

User-supplied Jira dashboard images and exports establish that, by 2024 and early 2025, a structured `i360 Bugs & Development Queue` was in use with Joe Carroll listed as project lead. The dashboard views track issue intake and resolution by month and quarter, four-week completion velocity, request origin, cause, and affected portal tool. The source data includes seven monthly exports of resolved items classified as `User Error/Knowledge Gap` from July 2024 through February 2025, plus a four-week Kanban-velocity export. Those records preserve distinct fields for bug versus development request, field versus headquarters origin, affected tool, and cause classification.

This establishes a mature product-operations surface that could distinguish actual product defects from user knowledge gaps, show which parts of the portal generated demand, and measure delivery flow. The records do not identify who designed the dashboards, who owned every workflow, or whether the measures caused a specific service improvement.

The dashboard screenshots show that the system covered a broad set of portal capabilities, including CRM, events, survey tools, field applications, action centers, and calling. They also show that the reporting surface differentiated product defects from user-error or knowledge-gap reports. This supports the account that the work combined product planning, user feedback, implementation support, and training signals.

## V4 product and implementation model

The core metadata of `Portal V4 Regional Call Joe Carroll PPT.pptx` identifies Joseph Carroll as author and January 25, 2021 as its creation date. The deck describes the Data and Implementation Team as working with i360 on product development, helping states implement their visions through portal tools, incorporating other partners, and operating as a continuing-education resource. It describes the State Portal Contact role as expanding from bugs, training, and technical issues into product development, feedback, and V4 implementation.

The deck contrasts an earlier product-development approach that had limited field feedback, weak communication between organizations, and no early testing. It names the original Events Builder as an example and describes resulting technical debt and rework. Its V4 approach calls for field feedback, ongoing communication with i360, early testing, and iteration. A later slide states that feedback was central to avoiding problems associated with Events and child organizations. This directly corroborates that the organization had identified a need for a stronger business-to-delivery feedback process by early 2021. It does not establish Doug's authorship of the approach, the outcome of V4, the exact cause of earlier rollout problems, or a measured improvement.

It says the experience exposed the need for a dedicated role that could manage both daily interaction and the strategic relationship with the delivery team. The attached copy also includes embedded charts labeled `2018 Queue Data`, covering list-pull distribution by team member and cases submitted by month. These internal metrics remain in the source files and should not be copied into Git or public career material. The document also identifies a then-unfulfilled request to track part-time employees for the Political Team because of capacity limits. That corroborates a later unmet reporting need. ## Events rollout and external-integration account

Doug reports that an Events 1.0 rollout exposed the limits of a long requirements handoff without continuing product ownership. He recalls providing i360 a detailed requirements document, waiting roughly a year and a half for delivery, finding initial issues in a soft launch, and then seeing the broader rollout fail to operate as intended. He also recalls recurrent instability in an Eventbrite integration when upstream API changes disrupted it. He identifies these experiences as a key reason to establish a closer product-planning and delivery interface through Joe's Product Owner role.

This is a detailed account. The requirements document, launch record, incident record, rollout date, Eventbrite integration history, and exact scope remain unrecovered. Keep the specific failure, vendors, systems, and organizational context private unless a source record supports a safe, accurate account.

## What this source supports

- By 2024 and early 2025, the portal had a structured Jira-based bug and development queue with categorical reporting, delivery-flow monitoring, and a named project lead.
- The system separated defect reports from user-error and knowledge-gap cases, creating a feedback path between product planning and enablement.
- The dashboard recorded issue source, cause, tool area, intake, resolution, and delivery velocity. This is evidence of operational visibility, not proof of a particular improvement or customer outcome.
- Doug reports that he moved the work from informal bug tracking toward a dedicated Product Owner model, backed Joe Carroll's role change, and used early rollout and integration problems as the operating case for that change. Joe Carroll's January 2021 V4 deck corroborates the need for a stronger feedback, testing, and implementation model.
- The supplied scenarios-and-drivers copy corroborates a team model that separated Tier 1 portal support from more complex implementation work, documented the 2018 Events deployment as a product-operating turning point, and described substantial 2019-20 workload growth. Its original source and date still require recovery.
- Doug reports that the role required a business case for technical product ownership inside a grassroots organization, because central IT did not hold the portal's field context, demand, or prioritization responsibilities.

## Evidence boundary
