# AI-006 Evidence Addendum

## Source chain and timing

`Responsible AI Use.pdf`, last updated May 13, 2026, establishes the Stand Together community baseline. It names Microsoft Copilot, ChatGPT, and Claude as approved enterprise tools, directs staff away from personal or unapproved accounts for work, and describes escalation through supervisors, the AI Working Group, and application review.

AFP's `AI Governance, Risk and Responsible Use` working draft was created June 12 and last modified July 8. It is marked Internal Review and Not for Distribution. It states that the community policy sets a baseline, while AFP needs additional specificity because of its multi-entity tax structure, political sensitivity, and distributed execution model. It separates individual use from building AI-assisted tools or workflows for others. It introduces decision rights, structured experiment criteria, API and external-connection questions, risk categories, and a supervisor role.

The five guidance documents were saved in Word, PDF, and HTML forms between June 23 and June 26. Their titles run from how AI changes a role, through AFP's AI rationale, practical uses, prompting and context practices, and data and information handling. The HTML assets establish that short-form interactive versions were prepared. Current records do not independently establish their live publication on ShareTogether.

## Vision and pilot integration

The series is materially more than a rule against personal accounts. Part 1 states that it was built on AFP's AI vision, discovery findings, and ChatGPT Enterprise pilot. It uses qualitative pilot examples about coverage, verification, cognitive load, recurring work, and the shift from initial production toward editorial judgment. Part 2 frames AFP's approach around empowerment, problem clarity, human judgment, accountability, shared intelligence, purpose, and ethical use. Part 5 makes the approved-enterprise-tool distinction explicit, then carries forward human judgment, entity awareness, and review responsibilities for outputs.

The documents establish the use of qualitative pilot findings and use cases. They do not establish that Doug had sufficient product-usage analytics to guide the package at a detailed level.
