# Notion Intake for Career Recovery

Use Notion to find the work that has a durable record, not to bulk-copy a personal knowledge base into a resume exercise. Start with a workspace or project area likely to contain finished artifacts, project databases, decision records, work plans, or retrospectives. Work one cluster at a time.

## Access and scope

When a Notion connection is available, first identify the few databases, index pages, or project hubs most likely to contain career-relevant work. When it is not available, ask the user for a page link, export, or short list of candidate project areas. Do not imply that a page has been inspected when it has not been supplied or accessed.

Keep the review selective. A representative set of strong projects, role transitions, and artifacts is more useful than an exhaustive archive. Stop once the evidence log contains a credible picture of the recent role and the highest-value gaps are clear.

## What to extract

For each useful page or database entry, capture the page title, URL or export reference, approximate date, project or workstream, and the evidence fields in the Career Evidence Model. Read the page for the candidate’s actual contribution, what became tangible, who used or decided something, and what result can be observed.

Page authorship, a timestamp, an edit history, and a task assignment can establish chronology or point toward a source. They do not by themselves establish that the candidate owned the work or produced the outcome. Confirm those claims through the page’s content, a linked artifact, feedback, or the user’s account.

## Sensitivity and synthesis

Treat internal strategy, stakeholder names, draft policy, budgets, and unpublished metrics as internal evidence by default. Translate the mechanism and type of result into externally safe language before using it in a resume or LinkedIn profile.

A useful output from a Notion pass is a compact candidate-evidence list with three labels: confirmed, needs confirmation, and do not use externally. Preserve the original page reference so that later drafting and interview preparation can return to the underlying work rather than relying on a polished summary.
