# Using Doug's Judgment Profile in Career Work

Doug's supplied judgment profile is a behavioral reference. It describes how he forms positions, evaluates quality, communicates under pressure, and benefits from a thought partner. It is useful career context. It is not evidence that a resume claim is accurate, a formal title is warranted, or an employer observed a result.

## What to use

Start with a concrete draft, a working career map, or a claim ledger for Doug to react to. Questions about a specific situation will produce better evidence than broad prompts for self-description. Help him notice pattern repetition across roles, but test every pattern with actual stories.

The profile suggests candidate capability hypotheses including systems thinking, pattern recognition across contexts, practical use of frameworks, and a builder instinct. Treat each as a hypothesis. Ask for episodes from different roles that reveal the mechanism, what Doug personally did, the result, and the limit of the claim.

The profile also identifies a confidence gap, a tendency toward cautious framing, and a need for concise preparation before live conversations. Use those observations to ask whether a claim is being understated, whether caution is evidence-based or self-protective, and how to express a defensible position aloud. Do not compensate for uncertainty by exaggerating ownership, scope, or impact.

## What not to use

Do not convert the profile's north star into a resume title or assume that a stated aspiration is a demonstrated qualification. Do not treat its positive assessment of Doug's judgment as independent corroboration of work results. Do not surface confidential AFP context from the profile in external-facing materials.

A resume claim still needs a specific contribution, an honest evidence basis, and language that a knowledgeable colleague could defend. At each point, distinguish what Doug remembers, what an artifact or colleague supports, and what the profile merely helped us notice.
